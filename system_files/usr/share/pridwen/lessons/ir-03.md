# Contain and account

Once you understand enough, containment stops the situation from spreading
without destroying what you have preserved. The cleanest containment for a
networked host is to cut it off while leaving it running, so its memory and
connections remain for study.

`sudo firewall-cmd --panic-on` drops all traffic in both directions, isolating a
Range host on the spot; `--panic-off` restores it. That is gentler than powering
down, which loses volatile state, and more complete than killing one connection.
If a specific account is the vector, locking it with `sudo passwd -l name` and
ending its sessions with `loginctl terminate-user` stops that path.

```
$ sudo firewall-cmd --panic-on          # isolate, keep it running
$ sudo passwd -l webadmin               # lock a compromised account
$ sudo loginctl terminate-user webadmin
```

The deliverable from the whole exercise is an account: an ordered timeline of
what happened, what you did, and when, built from the captures you took. On the
Range this is graded like a report, and the habit carries directly into real
work, where the timeline and the record of your own actions are what let someone
else pick up the case. Contain to stop the bleeding, then write it down.

## Try it

1. On a Range host, isolate it with panic mode and confirm connections are cut.
2. Lock a suspect account and end its sessions.
3. Assemble your captures into a short ordered timeline.
4. Explain why isolation is preferred over powering the host off.
